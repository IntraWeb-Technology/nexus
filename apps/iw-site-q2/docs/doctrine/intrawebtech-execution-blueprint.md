**Status:** Strategic planning. Pre-copywriting. Visitor-centered.**

## How to Use This Document

This blueprint covers five execution areas in sequence:
1. Homepage restructuring
2. Work/Proof system architecture
3. Operator trust layer expansion

5. Implementation visibility

Each section specifies: **what changes**, **why**, **structural direction**, and **what must not change**.

## Section 1: Homepage Restructuring

### The Core Problem to Solve

The current homepage leads with worldview and asks the visitor to earn their way to the practical value. The fix is sequencing, not repositioning. The operational infrastructure framing stays — it moves to a reinforcement position, not the opening position.

### Required Sequence Shift

**Current order:**
1. Philosophy (operational infrastructure language)
2. Friction list (recognition)
3. Proof (results)
4. Process (model)
5. Fit (who it's for)
6. CTA

**Target order:**
1. Practical consequence — what disappears from the visitor's operation
2. Recognition — friction patterns that confirm they're in the right place
3. Proof — what changed for others like them
4. Infrastructure framing — *now* the worldview lands as reinforcement, not introduction
5. Process — what working together actually involves
6. Fit — who this is and isn't right for
7. CTA — with an outcome promise, not a process description

### Hero Section Direction

The hero needs to answer one question in under four seconds: "What does this company remove from my operation?"

**Structural direction:**
- Primary statement: one sentence naming the operational burden that disappears
- Secondary statement: the mechanism (systems, automation, infrastructure)
- The phrase "without adding headcount" should be in the primary statement, not buried in the subhead
- Remove any sentence that requires the visitor to hold an abstract concept before receiving a practical one

**What must not change:**
The restrained, non-hypey register. No outcome inflation. No startup energy. The tone stays.

### Friction Ticker Direction

The scrolling ticker earns recognition but loses the conversion moment. Structural fix:

- Replace the animated ticker with a static grid (3x3 or 2x4)
- Each pain point gets one short sentence of consequence appended — not just the label
- Example direction: "Systems don't talk. People do." becomes "Systems don't talk. People do. Every day someone manually bridges the gap."
- The consequence sentence makes the pain kinetic, not just named

**What must not change:**
The specificity of the pain points. These are accurate and differentiated. Do not genericize them.

### Proof Cards Direction (Homepage)

The three cards (Request to Fulfillment, Monthly Reporting, Onboarding) are the strongest trust moments on the site. Structural additions needed:

- Each card adds one line of context: anonymous company type, size, and vertical
  - Direction: "A 40-person distribution company" or "A professional services team of 60"
  - This one line does the job of a case study header without requiring named attribution
- Each card adds one sentence after the impact stat describing what operationally changed — not just the number
  - Direction: "The team stopped manually chasing status and started seeing it in real time."
- Keep the "What Changed" / "Impact" structure exactly as-is — it works

### Process Section Direction (Model)

The four steps (Diagnose, Design, Implement, Optimize) are currently invisible as differentiators. Structural fix:

- Each step gets a concrete output description — one sentence naming what exists at the end of that step that did not exist before
  - Diagnose: output is a documented map of actual workflow vs. assumed workflow, with identified breakpoints and ownership gaps
  - Design: output is a prioritized infrastructure plan with clear scope, sequence, and dependency logic
  - Implement: output is running, monitored systems delivered in phases — not documentation
  - Optimize: output is a performance baseline against which improvements are tracked

- One line under each step clarifies what the visitor's role is at that stage — this starts building implementation imagination without a separate section

### Who We Work With Direction

- Add one sentence per fit indicator that makes the consequence concrete
  - "Teams of 20-150 people" is a filter, not a reason to care
  - Direction: "Teams of 20-150 people — large enough for operational complexity to hurt, small enough that adding headcount to fix it isn't realistic"
- The animated workflow diagram needs a one-line label identifying what it represents
  - Direction: a small caption — "A lead intake workflow built for a services company with three routing paths" — transforms it from tech theater into proof of implementation

### CTA Section Direction

Replace the three process reassurances with one outcome promise plus one expectation-setter.

- Outcome promise: What the visitor will understand after the call that they don't understand now
- Expectation-setter: One honest sentence about what the call is and is not

The current "No sales pitch / Strategic conversation / Clarity on next steps" trio is reassuring but not motivating. A motivated visitor doesn't need to be told it won't be a pitch. They need to be told what they'll walk away with.

## Section 2: Work/Proof System Architecture

### The Strategic Gap

The Work page is currently an empty placeholder while every other page on the site links to it. This is the site's most urgent structural problem. Beyond filling it, the proof architecture needs a narrative format that supports the operational infrastructure positioning — traditional case studies don't.

### Proposed Proof Narrative Structure

Name this section something precise and owned — not "Case Studies." Consider: "Operational Records" or "What Changed" (already used on homepage cards — extend it).

Each entry follows this structure:

**1. Operational Situation**
One paragraph. Company type (anonymous), size, vertical. What their operation looked like before contact. Written as operational description, not as a client complaint.

**2. What Was Breaking**
Specific, enumerated. Not "inefficiency" — the actual breakpoints: where ownership changed, where data was re-entered, where a single person held a critical dependency, how long cycles actually took.

**3. Root Cause**
One to two sentences. The infrastructure gap beneath the symptom. This section is what demonstrates diagnostic depth — it differentiates IntraWeb from a vendor who takes requirements and builds.

**4. What Changed**
What was built. Named specifically. Not "we built an automation" — the actual systems connected, the logic implemented, the handoffs eliminated. Can reference tools by name (n8n, HubSpot, etc.) here — it builds implementation credibility without dominating the narrative.

**5. What Was Removed**
Enumerated. Specific manual steps, dependencies, or processes that no longer exist. This is the operational relief layer — the emotional payoff section. A visitor should be able to read this and feel the weight lifting.

**6. Measured Impact**
The number. One to three metrics. Anchored in the operational situation described above so they feel earned, not inflated.

**7. Ongoing Operational State**
One sentence describing what the client's operation looks like now on an ongoing basis. Not a quote. Not a testimonial. Just an operational description. "The reporting cycle now runs automatically every Monday. No one assembles it."

### Near-Term Work Page Fix

While full proof narratives are developed:

- Take the three homepage proof cards and expand each into the full narrative structure above
- These entries do not require client attribution — anonymous operational records are sufficient
- This transforms an empty page into a working proof system within one sprint

### Proof Page Metadata

Each entry should carry visible metadata tags:
- Industry vertical
- Company size range
- Primary service type (automation, integration, platform, etc.)
- Cycle time / before-and-after headline

This allows visitors to self-select entries relevant to their situation without reading everything.

## Section 3: Operator Trust Layer Expansion

### The Current State

The About page has five sentences about John Schibelli. No photo. No specific professional origin story. No named frustration. No sense of what 15 years in production engineering actually taught him that created this specific company.

For a firm where the value proposition is "the person you talk to is the person who does the work," this is the highest-ratio trust gap on the site — small change, large impact.

### What the Operator Section Needs

**A photo.** A real one. Not a logo. Not a geometric placeholder. A face creates the relationship before the call. This is not a nice-to-have.

**A specific origin narrative.** Not a career summary. Not a credentials list. One specific professional frustration that led to IntraWeb — the moment John saw the operational gap clearly and decided it was worth solving.

Structural direction for the narrative:
- Name the frustration concretely: what kept breaking, what kept getting built wrong, what the industry kept missing
- Connect it directly to the IntraWeb model: this is why the diagnostic precedes the build, this is why implementation visibility is non-negotiable, this is why ongoing continuity is part of the engagement
- Keep it short — two to three paragraphs

**What it should not become:**
A founder's note. A manifesto. A personal brand pitch. This is an operational trust section. The reader should finish it understanding: this is someone who has seen this problem from the inside, thought carefully about it, and built a practice around solving it correctly.

**The "no account management" statement** currently buried in a quiet paragraph should be elevated to a featured element on the About page. It is the most emotionally resonant trust statement on the site.

**A visible stack disclosure.** The tools John builds with (n8n, HubSpot, Stripe, Clerk, Supabase, etc.) should appear on the About page — not as a logo parade, but as a brief operational statement. "The systems we build run on:" followed by a concise list. This grounds the abstraction in implementation reality.

### About Page Structural Sequence Fix

Current sequence on About: Position → What We Build → How Engagements Work → Why Different → What to Expect → Operator Background

Proposed sequence:
1. Operator and origin (lead with the person — this is who you're trusting)
2. What we build (now grounded in a person, not a brand)
3. Why the approach is different (with the comparison table — keep it)
4. How engagements work (with step-level output descriptions)
5. What to expect (keep as-is — it's good)
6. CTA

## Section 4: Operational Relief Messaging

### The Gap

The current site earns intellectual trust — a visitor understands IntraWeb is sophisticated and serious. It does not yet earn emotional certainty — the visitor does not yet feel their specific operational burden getting lighter.

The distinction: intellectual trust answers "do they know what they're doing?" Emotional certainty answers "will my operation feel different if I work with them?"

The site currently answers the first question. It needs to answer the second.

### What Operational Relief Messaging Is Not

- It is not testimonials with sentimental language
- It is not "imagine the possibilities" marketing copy
- It is not emotional manipulation or hyperbole

### What It Is

Operational relief messaging describes the concrete removal of specific burdens. It is specific enough to be credible. It is outcome-framed enough to be emotionally activating.

The proof cards already do this well when they say "5 handoffs eliminated." The site needs more moments like that, throughout the scroll, in plain operational language.

### Where Relief Messaging Needs to Appear

**Hero:** The primary statement should name what disappears — not what gets built. "Your operations stop depending on whoever is around" is relief messaging. "We build operational infrastructure" is not.

**Friction Ticker / Grid:** Each pain point gets a consequence appended. The consequence is the relief setup — it makes the pain more real so the resolution lands harder.

**Process Steps:** Each step's output description should name what the visitor no longer has to do, not just what IntraWeb delivers. "After the Diagnostic, you have a clear view of exactly where your operation is losing time — and you stop guessing."

**Proof Entries (What Was Removed section):** This is the primary relief delivery point. It should be the most emotionally resonant section of each proof entry. Be specific and enumerated: what manual step no longer exists, what dependency was broken, what anxiety was removed.

**CTA Section:** The call-to-action should end with a relief statement, not a process statement. The visitor should finish reading it imagining what their Monday looks like when the friction is gone — not thinking about what the first call will feel like.

### Relief Messaging Vocabulary Constraints

Words and phrases to use: removed, eliminated, no longer, stopped depending on, runs automatically, visible, predictable, stable, maintained

Words and phrases to avoid: transform, streamline, leverage, unlock, supercharge, seamlessly, game-changing, revolutionize

The vocabulary should match the register of someone describing an operational environment that works — not someone selling a product.

## Section 5: Implementation Visibility

### The Gap

A visitor with genuine interest cannot currently picture what happens after they book the call. The engagement is described in phases but not in human terms — what they do, what they provide, what they receive at each step, how long each step takes, and what their operation looks like while work is in progress.

This is "operational imagination" — the visitor needs to be able to run a mental simulation of working with IntraWeb before they commit to the first conversation.

### The Engagement Reality Layer

This does not require a new page. It can live on About, Diagnostic, or as an expandable section on the homepage. The content is:

**After the first call:**
What was discussed. What they receive — even informally. What happens next. Timeline expectation.

**During the Diagnostic:**
Who participates. What sessions look like. What access is needed and why. Approximately how long. What the deliverable looks like when they receive it.

**During Implementation:**
How progress is communicated. How disruption to existing operations is managed. What "phases" means practically — what they receive at the end of each phase and what they can use immediately. What ongoing access looks like.

**After Implementation:**
What the retainer relationship involves. How the system is monitored. How they raise issues. How evolution is managed as the business scales.

### Structural Direction

This content works best as a timeline or phase-by-phase narrative — not as bullet points. The format should feel like an honest conversation about what to expect, not a service description.

The About page's "What to Expect" section is the right container for this — it already exists and has the right heading. It currently has five bullets that describe IntraWeb's working style but not the engagement reality. Expand this section significantly.

### The 62 Workflows Stat

This number — currently buried in a Diagnostic FAQ — should be visible above the scroll threshold on at least one primary page (homepage, services, or diagnostic).

Placement direction: near the proof section on the homepage, presented as an operational statement, not a boast.

Format direction: "62 production workflows currently running across 8 operational categories" followed by a brief list of the categories. This is implementation visibility and trust evidence simultaneously.

## What Must Not Change

- The operational infrastructure positioning and its vocabulary
- The "before it's yours to own" diagnostic approach
- The restraint and anti-hype register throughout
- The "What Changed" structure in proof cards
- The "It's not for you if" qualifier on the Diagnostic page
- The "no account management layer" trust statement (elevate it, don't remove it)
- The comparison table on About (Why the Approach is Different)
- The e-commerce section on Services — it is the writing model for the rest of the site

## Anti-Drift Guardrails

Three risks to actively manage as copy gets written:

**Risk 1: Manifesto drift.** Every section that explains the "why" behind the approach risks becoming philosophical. The test: if a sentence can be removed without losing a concrete fact, it is probably manifesto material. Cut it or convert it.

**Risk 2: AI agency collapse.** As operational relief messaging gets written, there is a gravitational pull toward "AI automation" language. The differentiator is not that IntraWeb uses AI — everyone does. The differentiator is the diagnostic-first, infrastructure-layer, operational-understanding approach. That framing must survive every rewrite.

**Risk 3: Simplification past the credibility floor.** Some complexity is load-bearing. The operational infrastructure framing, the diagnostic-precedes-scope model, the "running systems not frameworks" distinction — these are not jargon. They are what makes IntraWeb specific. Simplify the delivery. Preserve the substance.
