# 03 — Environmental Doctrine
**IntraWeb Technologies — Page-Level Visual Atmosphere**
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Governing — applies to all page-level visual and layout decisions

---

## Scope

This document defines the visual atmosphere, spatial behavior, and environmental logic of the IntraWeb homepage. It governs decisions about spacing, contrast, density, motion, typography, and scan behavior at the page level.

This document does not govern individual snapshot construction (see `02-snapshot-doctrine.md`) or section-level production constraints (see `05-proof-section-governance.md`).

---

## Environmental Purpose

The page environment must communicate operational competence before the visitor reads a single word.

This does not mean visual complexity. It means that the structure itself — how information is weighted, how sections transition, how space is used — signals systems thinking.

The visitor should subconsciously register: "These people understand operational consequence." That registration should happen through structural experience, not through claims.

---

## The "Seeing vs. Shown" Principle

**Shown:** The company controls what the visitor perceives, in what order, toward a predetermined conclusion. The visitor is guided through a persuasion environment.

**Seeing:** The conditions are present. The structure is legible. The visitor draws their own conclusion.

Every environmental decision is evaluated against this distinction. When a design element feels like it is directing the visitor toward a response, it has shifted toward showing. The target is an environment where the visitor feels they are encountering operational reality — not being presented a marketing story.

---

## Spacing Behavior

Spacing is functionally determined, not mathematically grid-derived.

Sections breathe where the content demands cognitive relief. Sections compress where density signals operational weight. The spacing pattern should feel responsive to content weight — not applied from a consistent spacing scale.

**Practical constraints:**
- High-friction sections (Friction list, Proof) run tighter
- Decompression sections (Model, CTA) run with more air
- Spacing variation between sections is intentional, not inconsistent
- Identical spacing values across all sections is a drift signal — it means a grid was applied rather than content weight respected

Spacing that looks mathematically perfect reads as designed. Spacing that varies with content reads as responsive to operational reality.

---

## Density Logic

The page should feel denser than a typical agency site and lighter than technical documentation.

Density comes from information weight, not visual decoration. A section feels heavy because the operational content is heavy — not because it has more visual elements.

**Density calibration by section:**
- Hero: moderate density — oriented but not overwhelming
- Friction: increasing density — list accumulates weight
- Argument: low visual density, high semantic weight — single idea, large treatment
- Pillars: moderate density — three parallel conditions
- Proof: highest density — compressed consequence plus diagram weight
- Model: reducing density — structured sequence, more space
- Continuity: low density — plain language, operational realism
- Filter: moderate density — two columns, controlled contrast
- CTA: lowest density — single action, maximum space

Density inversion — making a heavy section look light or a light section look heavy — produces cognitive dissonance. The visual weight should match the content weight.

---

## Contrast Behavior

High contrast on scan anchors. Low contrast on supporting body.

The hierarchy must be stark enough that a visitor scanning at speed never misreads which element is dominant. If the scan anchor and body copy compete visually, the hierarchy has failed.

**Dark band sections:**
The Argument section must feel like a genuine environmental shift — not a color accent. The contrast change should produce a brief reorientation: the visitor registers that they have moved into a different register, not just a different section.

If the dark band feels like a visual treatment applied to a section, it has been under-executed. It should feel like a different room.

---

## Asymmetry Tolerance

High asymmetry tolerance throughout.

The page uses a dominant left-rail structure. Elements are left-aligned as the default. Centered elements require specific justification.

The page should feel scan-path aware — built around how a headline scanner actually moves through a page, not around a centered, balanced layout grid.

Consistent bilateral symmetry reads as designed. Left-weighted asymmetry reads as information-structured.

---

## Image Restrictions

No photography on the homepage. No stock imagery. No hero images. No illustration.

The only visuals on the page are operational artifacts — the diagnostic snapshots in the Proof section and any structural diagrams in the Model section.

This is not an aesthetic constraint. Photography and illustration carry brand and lifestyle signals that conflict with the operational intelligence register. They shift the environment from a briefing to a presentation.

If a future stakeholder requests imagery, the constraint must be defended: adding imagery shifts the environment toward agency aesthetic and undermines the evidential register the page depends on.

---

## Motion Doctrine

Default position: no motion.

If motion exists, it must read as a system state change — not as an animation.

**Permitted:**
- Scroll-entry diagram reveal — a path or node becoming visible as a section enters the viewport, if and only if it reads as a system condition revealing itself, not as an entrance animation

**Forbidden:**
- Continuous loops or pulses on any element
- Entrance animations that call attention to themselves
- Transitions between sections that produce an aesthetic response
- Motion that a motion designer would describe as "elegant" or "satisfying"

**The motion test:**
If someone watching the page says "that's a nice animation" — the motion has failed. Motion should be so restrained it goes nearly unnoticed. If it is noticed and commented on positively as animation, it is too much.

---

## Typography Hierarchy

Three active type levels at any point on the page:

1. **Scan anchor** — dominant, readable at arm's length, carries the section's primary communication
2. **Body** — readable at normal screen distance, carries supporting operational content
3. **Annotation** — smaller than body, used within diagrams and for subordinate operational specificity

A fourth level — **system label** — appears in JetBrains Mono within diagram contexts only.

**Rules:**
- No fourth level introduced into the page body without authorization
- Scan anchor and body must have stark hierarchy — subtle hierarchy fails at scan speed
- System labels in monospace signal operational data, not marketing copy
- No bold treatment on annotation labels — bold implies hierarchy within annotations, which flattens evidential weight

---

## Scan-Speed Doctrine

The page must be legible at three simultaneous speeds.

**Three-second scan:** Headline and scan anchors only. A visitor who spends three seconds on the page should carry away the company's core position.

**Fifteen-second scan:** Friction list items, consequence callouts, section transitions. A visitor who spends fifteen seconds should understand the company's operational territory and feel the first recognition response.

**Full read:** Body copy, diagram annotations, CTA reasoning. A visitor who reads fully should feel consequence pressure, structural clarity, and procedural confidence at the CTA.

Each speed must produce a complete — if compressed — understanding. The page cannot require full reading to communicate its position. If the fifteen-second scan produces confusion, the scan anchor system has failed.

---

## Anti-Polish Philosophy

Over-polish reduces trust in this specific context.

A page that looks too refined signals that the company's energy went into presentation rather than operational substance. For a buyer who thinks in systems, excessive visual polish carries the same negative signal as an overly designed slide deck in a technical meeting.

**Specific signals that cleanup has gone too far:**

- All sections feel visually equivalent in weight and treatment
- Spacing is mathematically perfect and consistent
- Diagrams are symmetrical and resolved
- Motion is smooth and satisfying
- The overall design could be described as "clean" or "polished" without qualification

**The target response:**
"This feels like it was built by people who understand what they're doing." Not: "This is a beautiful website."

The distinction matters. The first response signals operational credibility. The second signals design investment. This buyer values the first and is mildly suspicious of the second.

---

## Environmental Rupture Logic

The page has one deliberate environmental rupture: the Argument section (dark band).

This is not a design accent. It is a structural interruption. The visitor is moving through accumulating recognition pressure in the Friction section and encounters a completely different visual environment. The rupture signals: the register has changed.

**What the rupture must accomplish:**
- Feel like a different room, not a different section
- Deliver a single structural idea at maximum weight
- Provide cognitive stabilization — not more pressure, a reorientation
- Return to the base environment cleanly after the band ends

**What the rupture must not be:**
- A decorative band
- A "highlight" section with a dark background
- A visual break for aesthetic rhythm
- One of several color-band sections on the page

There is one rupture. It earns its existence by delivering the philosophical hinge of the page. A second dark band section would dilute the first.

---

## Section Transition Logic

Transitions between sections are tonal and structural, not animated.

The shift from Friction to Argument: register change — the list ends, a single idea expands.
The shift from Proof to Model: density change — compressed evidence resolves into legible sequence.
The shift from Filter to CTA: pressure release — qualification complete, procedural step follows.

Transitions communicate structural meaning. They are not visual connectors. Animated transitions between sections — slide, fade, reveal — shift the environment toward presentation and away from evidence.

---

## What Makes the Page Feel Too Polished

- Every section uses the same spacing unit
- Visual elements align to a visible grid
- Diagrams look like they came from a design system template
- Section backgrounds are color-coded by content category
- Motion is smooth and professionally executed
- The overall page could be submitted to a design awards competition

If any of these are present, the page has prioritized visual craft over operational register. Revise before build.

---

## What Makes the Page Feel Sterile

- No variation in section density
- Body copy and scan anchors have insufficient contrast
- Diagrams are too sparse to carry evidential weight
- Whitespace is applied uniformly rather than functionally
- The page feels like a minimalist portfolio rather than an operational environment

Sterility is the over-correction from anti-polish. The page should not feel empty. It should feel weighted — with information that earns its presence.

---

## What Makes the Page Feel Emotionally Dead

- No tension accumulation through Friction
- No register shift at the Argument band
- Proof section carries no consequence weight
- Model section feels like a services list rather than structural resolution
- CTA feels like a generic contact form prompt

Emotional deadness is produced by correct visual execution without scroll psychology. A page can follow every environmental rule and still feel inert if the pacing architecture is absent. See `04-scroll-psychology.md` for the pacing layer.

---

## What Creates Operational Rhythm Without Marketing Aesthetics

Rhythm in this context comes from:

- **Density variation** — sections alternate between information-heavy and structurally simple
- **Type scale shifts** — scan anchor scale changes signal register transitions
- **The dark band rupture** — single environmental interruption resets the visitor's cognitive state
- **Diagram visual variety** — different failure geometries across the Proof section create internal visual rhythm without stylistic variation
- **Left-edge weight variation** — scan anchor positions vary in vertical weight, creating rhythm along the primary scan path

Rhythm that comes from color, animation, or decorative visual elements is marketing aesthetics. Rhythm that comes from content weight variation is operational pacing. The first signals a designed presentation. The second signals a structured briefing.
